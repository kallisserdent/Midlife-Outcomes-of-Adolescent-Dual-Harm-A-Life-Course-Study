# Midlife Outcomes of Adolescent Dual Harm: A Life Course Study
## Project Abstract 
Adolescent self-harm and harm toward others are individually associated with poor outcomes, but few studies have examined the midlife outcomes of their co-occurrence. We tested associations of adolescent dual harm with midlife outcomes and evaluated the extent to which associations were explained by early-life risk factors. Participants (N=7,590, median age=14.8y, 49.0% male, 73.5% White, 10.6% Hispanic) were from the National Longitudinal Study of Adolescent to Adult Health. Self-harm and harm toward others were assessed in grades 7-12 using past-year suicide attempt and items from a delinquency scale, respectively. Outcomes were assessed at ages 32-42 through indicators of mental health, physical health, social health, and socioeconomic well-being. Early-life risk factors included low self-control, childhood maltreatment, and low cognitive ability. At midlife, relative to adolescents with neither harm, those with dual harm exhibited poorer functioning across all outcome domains. Relative to adolescents with self-harm only, those with dual harm had elevated risk of property loss and elevated risk of victimization (though this difference was not statistically significant). After accounting for early-life risk factors, the association with property loss was attenuated and the association with victimization was strengthened. Adolescents with dual harm have elevated risk for poor midlife functioning, warranting early prevention efforts.

## Table of Contents 
### Step 1 - Develop Analytic Cohort 
Merging raw study variables from Add Health Waves I, II, III, and V. Keeping participants who have data in Wave V.  

### Step 2 - Dual Harm Groups 
Identifying whether participants have self-harm or harm towards other in adolescence. 

### Step 3 - Midlife Outcomes 
Deriving midlife outcome variables, including variables on mental (suicide attempt, depression symptoms, substance misuse), physical (self-rated health, chronic health condition), social (marriage, victimization), and socioecontomic (unemployment, poverty, loss of property) well-being. 

### Step 4 - Covariates 
Deriving covariates including age, sex, race, ethnicity, adolescent socioeconomic status, adolescent self-control, childhood maltreatment, and adolescent cognitive ability. 

### Step 5 - Finalize Analytic Cohort 
Apply exclusions criteria to derive final analytic cohort. 

### Step 6 - Descriptive Statistics 
Output for Table 1 & Table 2. 

### Step 7 - Dual vs Neither Regressions 
Testing associations of adolescent dual harm with midlife outcomes, relative to those without self-harm or harm towards others. Output for Table 3 & Supplemental Table 2. 

### Step 8 - Dual vs Self Regressions

### Step 9 - Self vs Neither Regression

### Step 11 - Other vs Neither Regressions
